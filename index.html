Schools & Students

Safe AI habits, in your classroom

A small, low-risk pilot that teaches teens to use generative AI safely, check what it tells them, and use it to learn.

Why now

Schools are looking for safe AI

CBSE's computational thinking and AI curriculum for Classes 3 to 8 begins in 2026-27 and includes ethics and responsible AI. The question is how to teach it well.

  • Students will meet AI tools with or without the school. Teaching safe habits early is better than leaving them to learn alone.
  • A hands-on pilot lets the school evaluate the idea with minimal risk before any wider commitment.
What students learn

Six habits for using AI well

How AI works

How a generative AI picks its words, and why that makes it sometimes wrong.

Clear prompts

Asking with a task, context and format, then improving the result.

Checking facts

Spotting confident wrong answers and verifying them with a reliable source.

Bias and fairness

Noticing whose voices can be missing from what AI has learned.

Privacy

Knowing what personal information never to type into an AI tool.

Learning, not copying

Using AI to understand and self-test, and doing the final work themselves.

How it works

Learning by doing

1. Try it together

Short activities, many of them without a screen, followed by guided practice on the projector.

2. Check and reflect

Students keep a fact-check log and a short journal, so they practise questioning what AI says.

3. Show what they learned

Teams finish with a project and a short showcase for the teacher and guests.

Built with safety in mind

How we protect students

  • Only age-appropriate tools, and only those the school approves.
  • No personal information is entered into any AI tool.
  • A short note goes to parents, and consent is taken before any photos or student work are shared.
  • Every session has a version that works without internet.
